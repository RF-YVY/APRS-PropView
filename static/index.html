Welcome to APRS PropView

Step 1 of 6

See what VHF APRS is doing

PropView shows who your station hears, how packets reached you, and whether recent direct RF paths suggest improving VHF conditions.

Good starting point: configure reception first. Transmit features are optional and remain under your control.

1. Identify your station

In Settings → Station & Location, enter your callsign and accurate station coordinates. These coordinates anchor distances, bearings, propagation paths, weather, and watched targets.

  • Use the map picker when you do not know the coordinates.
  • Choose Imperial or Metric under Map & Display.

2. Connect a receive source

Add a serial or TCP TNC under Radio & TNC. APRS-IS can provide regional context, but direct RF reception is the strongest evidence for conditions at your station.

Before enabling transmit: verify your callsign, RF path, passcode, frequency, and local operating requirements. Use packet previews first.

3. Read the map correctly

  • Direct RF paths run straight from the transmitting station to you.
  • Relayed RF paths pass through known digipeaters.
  • APRS-IS stations provide network context and do not prove that your radio heard them.

Use the Direct RF control when evaluating reception at your own antenna.

4. Turn observations into decisions

The two propagation meters separate direct reception at your station from broader regional RF activity. Click a meter to inspect its evidence and freshness.

Enable Grid on the map, choose a Maidenhead square, enter a target station or area label, and add it as a watched path.

5. You are ready to observe

Let PropView collect a normal baseline before tuning propagation alerts. The alert helper becomes more useful after about 24 hours of representative RF history.

You can reopen this guide at any time from About/Help → Illustrated Quick Start or from the full Help & User Guide.

📖 APRS PropView — Help & User Guide

Overview

APRS PropView is a real-time APRS digipeater and Internet Gateway (IGate) application focused on visualizing VHF propagation conditions. It decodes APRS packets from RF (via a TNC) and APRS-IS, plots stations on a live map, draws signal paths, scores propagation quality, and adds weather radar plus configurable NWS alert overlays for faster storm awareness.

Watch the APRS PropView video guide

🗺️ Map

The central map shows your station (blue dot), RF stations (red border), APRS-IS stations (blue border), saved APRS objects, and optional weather overlays. Propagation lines animate between heard stations with directional arrows and can be color-coded by distance:

  • Red — < 31 mi (50 km)
  • Orange — 31–62 mi (50–100 km)
  • Green — 62–124 mi (100–200 km)
  • Purple — > 124 mi (200 km) — DX!

Small white dots along a path indicate known digipeater hops. Direct-heard stations draw a line straight to your station; digipeated stations draw through known digipeater waypoints. If a used digipeater position is unknown, no line is drawn so the contact is not mistaken for direct reception.

If a moving station was last heard by your RF station and later appears from APRS-IS at a newer, different position, PropView removes the stale RF map marker and keeps the newer network position so one callsign does not appear in two places.

🎛️ Map Controls

📏Toggle Lines — Show/hide propagation path lines.

Weather radar and NWS polygons are managed from Settings - Weather. While APRS object creation is active, alert polygons temporarily stop intercepting clicks so any point inside a watch or warning remains selectable; normal alert popups resume after placement or cancellation. Base map tiles are served through PropView's tile proxy, so cached default OSM or custom XYZ tiles can be reused when the upstream tile source is unavailable. All map toggle states, zoom level, position, and filter selections are saved automatically and persist across restarts. Expanded weather alert banners rise above map controls and scroll when alert text is long; use the X on an alert banner to clear that alert from view.

Application update checks are managed from Settings - Web Interface. You can disable release checks entirely or choose how often the app rechecks GitHub while it stays running. Windows setup installs can download and launch newer setup installers from the About tab; Linux, Raspberry Pi, and macOS installs show release notices without the Windows-only installer button.

📻 RF Stations & 🌐 APRS-IS Tabs

Scrollable lists of stations heard on RF or via the APRS-IS network. Each entry shows the station's APRS icon, callsign, distance, heading, last heard time, and packet count. Use the filter dropdowns to narrow by time window, distance range, station type, source, callsign text, and direct/via-digipeater RF path.

📦 Packets Tab

A live feed of decoded APRS packets. Each entry shows the source (RF port, APRS-IS, transmitted packet, or RF digipeat), timestamp, raw packet contents, and decoded fields. Use the source filter to show packets digipeated by your station, choose newest-first or oldest-first sorting, or clear the local packet list.

💬 Messages Tab

Send and receive APRS messages. Type a destination callsign and message text (max 67 characters), then click Send. Incoming messages appear with ack/rej status indicators. Click any received message's callsign to auto-fill the To field for a quick reply.

Messages addressed to sibling SSIDs can optionally appear in the inbox. For example, a station running as W1AW-1 can show messages sent to W1AW-7 or W1AW-9; only exact station-ID messages are auto-ACKed.

Use the sort selector to view newest-to-oldest or oldest-to-newest. The mobile UI includes the same ordering behavior and a manual refresh button.

Message notification channels (Discord, Email, SMS) are configured separately from band-opening alert channels in Settings → Alerts.

📊 Propagation Tab

Real-time propagation scoring with stat cards: RF stations (1h/6h/24h), max distance, average distance, and APRS-IS count. Includes a distance distribution histogram, a 24-hour propagation history chart, a VHF DXView link centered on your configured station location, and the Solar-Terrestrial source image.

📊 Analytics Tab

Advanced analysis subtabs:

  • Longest Paths — Leaderboard of the greatest distances reached.
  • Heatmap — Time-of-day vs. distance heatmap showing when propagation peaks.
  • Station Reliability — Which stations are heard most consistently.
  • Best Times — Hourly chart showing the best time of day for propagation.
  • Sporadic-E — Select 6h, 24h, or 7d windows and review RF station counts, strongest stations, candidate gates, and path-weighted Es scores.
  • Weather Dashboard — Pop out current weather gauges, records, charts, forecast tiles, and map context from the Weather Graphs section.
  • Alerts — History of band-opening alerts triggered by the system.

⚙️ Settings

All settings are saved to config.toml next to the executable. The category rail and search box replace the older single scrolling list. Modified sections pulse yellow, the sticky save action shows the number of changed sections, and each section can be restored to its last saved values.

After saving, PropView clearly reports whether settings applied immediately, require a browser refresh, or require a full application restart. Restart warnings name only settings whose values actually changed.

In Docker and TrueNAS installs, mount persistent storage to /data. Environment variables set in the container platform are startup overrides, so matching values saved in the web UI can be replaced when the app is stopped and started again.

The Overview category includes a first-run checklist, settings import/export buttons for safe experimentation, and a last-transmitted history panel for station, WXnow, status, MHeard, and weather-alert beacons.

Station

Your callsign, SSID, latitude/longitude (or use the 📌 map picker), APRS symbol, status comment, beacon interval (minutes), and beacon path (e.g. WIDE1-1). Use Preview Station Beacon to inspect the exact packet text before using Transmit Station Now.

Smart Beaconing

Smart beaconing uses GPS speed to choose between a slow/parked beacon interval and a faster moving interval. Enable GPS ingestion first, then set the slow interval, fast interval, and speed threshold for mobile or portable operation.

GPS Ingestion

GPS ingestion lets APRS PropView use a live position instead of only the saved station latitude/longitude. Turn on GPS Ingestion, choose a source, then choose whether GPS should only move your map marker or also update the station latitude/longitude fields.

LOCK prevents active GPS from changing the saved station latitude/longitude. Leave it enabled when you want GPS to move the map marker during mobile use without changing your configured home/base station position.

  • This browser/device - Uses browser geolocation from the current desktop or mobile browser. Use this when running the app from a phone, tablet, laptop, or remote browser that can provide location permission.
  • Own APRS position packets - Uses your own heard APRS position packets as the live GPS source. Use this when a radio/TNC, tracker, or Mobilink-style setup is already transmitting your current position into APRS PropView.
  • NMEA serial GPS - Reads NMEA GPS sentences from a local serial port such as a USB GPS puck, GPS-enabled TNC, or serial feed from another device.
  • NMEA TCP stream - Connects to a TCP host/port that provides NMEA sentences. Use this with network GPS bridges, companion software, or devices that expose GPS over TCP.
  • NMEA UDP listener - Listens for NMEA sentences sent to the configured UDP bind address and port. Use this when another app or device broadcasts GPS updates over the LAN.
  • gpsd daemon - Connects to a local or network gpsd service and consumes JSON TPV fixes. On most Linux/Pi systems gpsd listens on 127.0.0.1:2947.
  • Any source - Accepts the latest valid GPS fix from any supported source. This is useful for testing, mixed mobile/base setups, or fallback workflows, but selecting a specific source gives clearer status messages when a device is missing.

Move my map marker from GPS controls whether the live GPS fix moves your station marker on the map. Update station latitude/longitude also writes the live coordinates into Settings when unlocked; save the configuration if you want those coordinates kept after restart.

Digipeater

Enable/disable the digipeater. Configure which aliases to respond to (WIDE1-1, WIDE2-1) and the de-duplication window to prevent re-digipeating the same packet.

IGate

Enable/disable Internet gateway. Choose RF→IS (gate heard RF packets to APRS-IS), IS→RF (gate APRS-IS packets to local RF), or both.

APRS-IS

Server/port (default: rotate.aprs2.net:14580), your APRS-IS passcode, fixed or moving range filter mode, range in miles, and any extra filter expressions. Fixed stations generate filters like r/35/-79/80; moving/mobile clients can use m/80. Extra javAPRS filters such as b/CALL, t/poimqstunw, or -p/CW are appended as entered. Private/local APRS-IS-style servers that do not send a banner or logresp can still populate receive traffic; without login verification PropView keeps that connection read-only.

RF Ports

Add one or more named serial or TCP ports. Serial ports can use binary KISS frames or receive-only TNC2 monitor text for older radios. Kenwood TH-D7/TH-D7E users commonly need PACKET mode, 9600 bps PC speed, and Xon/Xoff flow control. Legacy single serial/TCP controls have been replaced by this multi-port list, but old saved config is imported automatically.

Tracking Blocklist

Use CALL-SSID to reject one station identifier or a base CALL to reject every SSID. Blocked packets are ignored before station, packet, message, propagation, and history updates.

Watched VHF Paths

Watched Paths alerts when direct-heard RF enhancement near the target station area reaches or exceeds the distance to a selected station. Stations heard through another digipeater are excluded. Enter a callsign and use Callook, HamDB, optional QRZ XML, or HamQTH lookup to populate a starting location, then correct the grid or coordinates if the operating site differs from the license address.

Select band, mode, frequency, and confidence, then enter local/target antenna height, transmit power, and antenna gain. PropView shows radio-horizon, EIRP, and capability context while still requiring matching live propagation evidence. Watched path lines can be shown or hidden from the map Line options.

Status, Dynamic, MHeard, and Weather Alert Beacons

Status/DX can transmit a compact propagation summary, rotate preset dynamic messages in sequence or randomly, send a direct-heard RF MHeard list, or beacon severe weather alert text when active. Use Preview Next Beacon and the weather-alert preview before one-shot transmit or enabling periodic beaconing.

Bulletins and APRS Objects

Scheduled bulletins send APRS BLN messages at a configured interval. Scheduled APRS objects transmit named object packets with position, symbol, comment, route, and path. Use previews before enabling periodic transmit, or use the map Obj control to create object entries from a clicked location.

WXnow Transmit

WXnow can send APRS weather packets from a local WXnow.txt, with optional position included in the packet. When positionless weather is selected, PropView can seed a matching weather-symbol position packet and then transmit the positionless WX report. Use Preview WXnow before Transmit Now.

Map & Display → Visualizations also lets you hide optional weather, lightning, alert, source-health, watched-path, and propagation-context panels for this browser.

Visualizations — Independently enable the propagation aura, new-path reveal, map color harmony, condition backdrops, expressive home marker, watched-path flow, quiet direct-heard activity moments, and Off/Basic/Enhanced live packet animation.

Units — Choose Imperial or Metric for distance, map weather temperature, APRS WX station temperature, wind, and precipitation displays.

Map Tiles — Use the default online OpenStreetMap tiles or point PropView at a custom XYZ tile URL such as a local MBTiles/XYZ server. Custom URLs must include {z}, {x}, and {y}.

Ghost After (min) — Stations not heard within this time will fade (pulsing dashed border, reduced opacity). Set to 0 to disable ghosting.

Expire After (min) — Stations not heard within this time are completely removed from the map. Set to 0 to never auto-expire. Ghost time should be shorter than expiry time so stations fade before disappearing.

Update Checks - Enable or disable automatic GitHub release checks and set how often the app should poll for a newer published version during long-running uptime.

Tracking

Max Station Age — How long (minutes) to keep a station in the database before cleanup removes it. Cleanup Interval — How often (minutes) to run the cleanup process.

Alerts — Band Opening Channels

Trigger an alert when propagation exceeds thresholds (min RF stations + min distance). Cooldown prevents repeated alerts. Quiet hours suppress alerts during specified times. Channels: Discord webhook, Email (SMTP), and SMS via email gateway.

The Alert Tuning Helper analyzes recent RF path history as rolling one-hour windows and recommends direct/regional station count, distance, and cooldown values that sit above your normal local baseline.

Use Send Test Alert to send a preformatted test message to selected Discord, Email, and SMS destinations without waiting for a real alert trigger.

Alerts — Message Notification Channels

Separate channel toggles for forwarding incoming APRS messages to Discord, Email, or SMS — independent from band-opening channels.

Weather

Enable weather with a US zip code or ICAO airport code. Current conditions use Open-Meteo. Alert sources can be automatic, Weatherbit with a user API key, or disabled. Auto uses NWS for US stations and Open-Meteo risk indicators outside the US.

When using WXnow as the current conditions source, the Open-Meteo condition fallback can still provide general condition text and icon while temperature, wind, humidity, and pressure come from your local station.

Weather Alert Distances - Banner range controls local warning banners, weather-alert beacons, and alert symbol behavior when using radius scope. Polygon range controls the wider map-only NWS alert overlay.

Map Overlays - Turn radar and alert polygons on or off, adjust radar opacity, enable radar animation, and choose which alert categories should be drawn on the map. When NWS alerts do not include native polygon geometry, PropView can use affected county/zone geometry as a fallback for drawable overlays.

Alert Polling - NWS county/zone UGC scope is available for US locations. Other alert sources use point-based or provider-defined coverage.

Adaptive Severe Weather Polling - When one of your configured trigger events, such as Tornado Watch or Severe Thunderstorm Watch, becomes active, alert polling automatically speeds up to the elevated interval so newly issued warnings are detected more quickly. After the trigger clears, polling stays elevated through the configured cooldown before returning to normal.

MQTT Integration

MQTT is for sending APRS PropView events and propagation status to other systems. It is not needed for normal APRS, digipeater, IGate, mapping, or messaging operation; enable it when you have an MQTT broker such as Mosquitto, Home Assistant, Node-RED, EMQX, or another dashboard/automation system that should receive PropView data.

Common use cases include showing current propagation level on a wall dashboard, triggering Home Assistant or Node-RED automations when a band opening alert fires, logging propagation metrics into another time-series system, or bridging PropView status to a shack display without scraping the web UI.

Configure the broker host, port, topic prefix, optional username/password, Home Assistant device identity, and optional watched callsigns. The default topic prefix is aprs/propview. APRS PropView publishes retained propagation metrics on aprs/propview/propagation, retained summary values on aprs/propview/score and aprs/propview/level, retained Home Assistant status on aprs/propview/ha/status, watched station state on aprs/propview/watched/<callsign>, and alert/event payloads on aggregate and typed event topics. MQTT reconnects live after saving settings.

📱 Mobile View

Access /mobile from a phone browser (e.g. via Tailscale) for a touch-optimized interface with Messages, Map, Stations, Propagation, and About tabs. The mobile Share GPS button can feed the phone's browser location into GPS ingestion when the source is set to This browser/device or Any source.

Using Tailscale or another VPN

To use the mobile companion away from your home network, run APRS PropView on your shack PC, Raspberry Pi, or VM and join that machine plus your phone to the same private VPN such as Tailscale. Set Settings → Web Interface → Host to 0.0.0.0, save, restart APRS PropView, then open http://TAILSCALE-IP:14501/mobile from the phone. Keep the port private to your VPN; avoid exposing the dashboard directly to the public internet.

For mobile GPS, set GPS source to This browser/device or Any source, open the mobile page over the VPN, tap Share GPS, and allow browser location permission. Leave LOCK enabled unless you intentionally want the phone GPS to overwrite saved station latitude/longitude.

⌨️ Tips

  • Units can be changed in Settings → Web Interface. The map legend distance toggle also switches the same Imperial/Metric preference.
  • All map UI preferences (toggles, filters, zoom, position, map theme) are saved automatically to your browser and restored on next launch.
  • The desktop UI light/dark theme is saved separately from the map light/dark theme.
  • Ghost + Expire work together: set Ghost to 60 min and Expire to 180 min, for example, so stations fade at 1 hour and vanish at 3 hours.
  • Auto-Fit zoom (🔎) will keep all visible stations in view and zoom back in as stations expire. It turns off when you manually pan or zoom — click the button again to re-enable.
  • The propagation score is calculated from RF station count, maximum distance, and average distance within the last hour.
  • If you want faster warning pickup for a surrounding county, switch alert scope from Station point to County/Zone UGC and enable elevated polling triggers in Settings → Weather.
  • For mobile operation, keep station latitude/longitude locked if you only want GPS to move your map marker. Unlock it only when you intentionally want the live GPS fix to update your saved station coordinates.
  • Use settings export before changing host, port, APRS-IS, or RF port settings so you can quickly restore a known-good configuration.
